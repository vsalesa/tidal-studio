Maestro
One teacher, three hats

It doesn't just make music. It teaches you to make it.

Every change to your song comes with its why. Little by little you go from clicking buttons to writing the code yourself.

Producer

It listens to your song and suggests concrete ideas, in the first person.

“I'd drop the kick for 8 bars out of every 16. When it comes back, it hits twice as hard. Try it and see what you think.”

Code teacher

Press «Explain» on any track and it tells you what each piece does.

“mask "<f!8 t!8>" is a switch per bar: 8 bars silent and 8 playing.”

Music teacher

Every idea teaches a concept: pulse, backbeat, major and minor, contrast…

“Minor chords sound melancholic; major ones, bright. Same rhythm, different color.”

Project wizard

Style, tempo, beat, bass, chords, melody and structure, by clicking options. You hear it while you choose.

Brainstorm

What do you want to feel? What place do you imagine? From your answers it designs your song plan with you.

Pre-listen

«Listen» plays each idea for a few bars and then returns to your version. Decide with your ears.

Code always visible

Every track is real TidalCycles code you can edit. No black boxes.

Your progress

See which code and music concepts you have used, and which are left to discover.

English and Spanish

Switch language with one button. Maestro answers in the language you choose.

See what's sounding

While it plays, the step that is sounding lights up in the code, the tracks and the blocks.

Mixing desk

Volume, 3-band EQ, mute and solo for every track, plus a Master. Your code stays clean, and a Mix tag on each track shows what it adds.

Skills and plugins

Maestro loads expert music skills and shows which ones it used. Add more, or whole Claude plugins, from a GitHub link.

How I decided

Each answer explains which skill guided each musical decision, and you can watch Maestro's reasoning live while it thinks.

AI detection and Diagnostics

It finds your Claude Code and how it's signed in. If Maestro doesn't answer, Diagnostics checks each step and tells you how to fix it.

A real app

Install it with everything inside and open it in its own window, with no Terminal. Closing it stops the sound engine.

Hear options, pick one

Ask Maestro for something and it offers 3 or 4 different takes. Listen to each one live over your song, switch A/B, and keep the one you like.

Timeline

See where every hit falls, bar by bar and beat by beat, instead of decoding "how often". TidalCycles itself calculates it, and a line follows the music.

Every hit points to its code

Hover a hit and the piece of code that makes it lights up. Hover the code and all its hits light up.

A plan, step by step

Ask for a whole song and Maestro first shows you a plan, like a brainstorm with a producer. Approve or change it, and it builds the song step by step, saving each one: if the AI takes too long, nothing is lost.

Maestro fetches the sounds

If your song needs a sound you don't have, like a real cowbell or a vinyl crackle, Maestro finds a free-licensed one online, downloads it, credits it and plays it. It asks before big packs.

A library for long loops

Drop downloaded loops, folders or a zip: the studio detects tempo, bars, key and hits, and writes the code so they play at your tempo, with variations instead of repeating.

600+ instruments

A harpsichord, a pizzicato double bass, a Steinway, a sitar… Browse by category with real photos, hear each one before downloading, add your own SoundFonts, and pin your favourites to the studio.

Open Tidal files in tabs

Open any .tidal or .hs from your disk or a song from GitHub, each in its own tab, and close them when you are done; Save writes a clean .tidal that plays in any Tidal editor.

    Genre courses

    Build a merengue from scratch.

    Eleven courses, one genre each: merengue, salsa, cumbia, reggaeton, bossa nova, jazz, funk, house, techno, drum & bass and lo-fi. Like a production class: the signature rhythm first, then bass, harmony, melody, arrangement and mix. Every step adds a piece that already sounds good, and Maestro explains why it sounds like that style. The Latin courses follow the Latin style skills of your composition plugin, name the tradition they play and use real instruments: güiro, congas, bongos, upright bass, trombones, accordion, nylon guitar… Nine courses also let you hear a real drummer play the style (MIDI from Google Magenta's Groove MIDI Dataset), with the strength and timing of every hit kept in the code.

    Import a song

    Start from a song you love. End with one of your own.

    Drop in a MIDI file, or an mp3 or wav (its instruments are separated with MSST or Demucs), and it becomes Tidal tracks plus its recipe: tempo, key, chords, structure and groove. Then Maestro helps you write something new inspired by it, keeping what works and changing the rest. Everything stays on your computer.

    Your MIDI keyboard

    Play your piano. Watch it become code.

    Plug in your USB keyboard and it sounds right away with any instrument in the studio. Record a phrase, in time with your song if it's playing, and it becomes Tidal tracks, one per hand, with the key and chords; the code is written while you play. Then Maestro explains what you played and suggests how to continue.

    localhost:8765
    Generate from your MIDI

    Your melody, as merengue. Or bossa, or house.

    Upload a MIDI of yours, pick one of eleven styles and what to keep: melody, chords, bass, drums or tempo. The studio writes the rest in that style over your chords, without AI: the course's drums, a bass and accompaniment with rhythms measured in real songs, and a new call-and-response melody if you don't keep yours. Every track says where it comes from, and Maestro can turn it into a whole song.

    Style recipes

    “Want it to sound like jazz?”

    Pick a style and your song transforms. You see the before and after with what's new highlighted, plus an explanation of every change, so tomorrow you can do it yourself.

    Before
    s "bd*4 , ~ cp ~ cp" n "<c'min7 f'min7>" # s "superpiano"
    After: jazz
    swingBy (1/3) 4 $ s "bd ~ [~ bd] ~ , ~ cp ~ cp" swingBy (1/3) 4 $ n "<c'min9 f'min9>" # s "superpiano"

    «swingBy (1/3) 4» delays every second note a little: that's swing. And the chords gain an extra note ('min9) to sound jazzier.

    Maestro v2 · your ear decides

    Lock what you love. Judge the rest by ear.

    Lock a whole track or just its rhythm, notes, sound, effects or mix, and the tempo: Maestro can't touch it, you still can. Vote between Maestro's options blind, by ear only; every vote stays on your computer as a dataset you can export. And a live monitor shows which of Maestro's layers answered each request, with its time and tokens; simple questions about your songs get answered without AI.

    Maestro v2 · the score

    Your song, also as sheet music.

    Every song is now .tidal code plus a MusicXML score with its melody, chords and bass. Maestro composes in the score first (motif, answer, climax, cadence) and the app turns it into Tidal. Open it in MuseScore, change it and bring it back. And on any course step, «Another version» gives you three new takes of the melody, bass or chords over the rest of your song.

    Maestro v2 · sounds that learn

    Pick sounds by ear. The studio learns.

    A Sound Lab measures how your sounds really sound (attack, decay, brightness, pitch, noise), only when you ask and all on your computer, bought packs included but never shared. Maestro only suggests sounds that play. Select a sound in your code and compare the best three by ear on the same phrase: the studio learns from your pick. Combine sound recipes, turn on an optional critic for groove, harmony and form, and try the experimental CLaMP 3 add-on.

    Maestro v2 · genres

    Fifteen genres. Cumbia, your way.

    Four new courses: reggae and dub, boom bap, afrobeat and bachata. Cumbia now asks which one you want before the rhythm: Colombian, sonidera, villera or chicha. Every course writes its form (intro, verse, mambo…) into the score, lets you choose each sound by ear and learns what you like in that genre, and warns you about sounds that don't play yet.

    Maestro v2 · memory and styles

    Maestro remembers you. You decide what.

    Tell it «remember that…» or just pick by ear: Maestro remembers it for this song, the course, the genre or always. What's clear is approved by itself; what's doubtful waits in an inbox, and every change can be undone. Save a song as a style (groove, roles, instruments and tempo, not the notes) and use it as inspiration, transform it to another key or tempo, or share it through GitHub. A storage panel shows what takes space and what happens if you delete it.

    0.19 · memory synced with GitHub

    Take your memory to another computer, or share it.

    Your approved memories, styles and cases sync with your GitHub repository in both ways. On a new computer, connect the same repository and everything comes back by itself; what you delete on one computer is deleted on the other, and every change can be undone. Each item says whether it's on GitHub, and you can compare before syncing. Paste someone else's repository link and their memories land in your inbox, so you choose which ones you want.

    Get started

    From zero to sound in three steps.

    You don't need anything installed. The installer detects your system and sets up SuperCollider, SuperDirt and TidalCycles.

    Mac, Windows and Linux: one installer, everything inside

    TidalCycles, SuperCollider, the sounds and Python are all in the package, so nothing is downloaded while installing. It opens as a real Mac app, in its own window and with no Terminal. The first time, macOS asks you to allow it in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.

    Windows installer (.exe) Linux package (.deb)
